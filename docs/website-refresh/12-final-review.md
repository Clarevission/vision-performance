# 12 — Final Corporate Website Review

Reviewed from five perspectives against the brief's question: *can this visitor understand VPI, and is it truthful about what is live?*

## 1. Employer (HR, operations, owner)

**Can an employer understand VPI?** Yes. The homepage says what VPI is in one line, shows the three areas of work, and routes to "Discuss a workplace vision program". Solutions pages explain *how* a program would run (steps, components, privacy boundary) without claiming services that don't exist. The Style Picker gives employers something concrete to do today.
**Gaps:** no pricing guidance (by decision), no case studies, and no named team to talk to. Employers may want a one-page program overview PDF (backlog CONT-3).

## 2. HSE leader

**Can an HSE leader understand SafetyOS and the occupational solutions?** Yes. SafetyOS is presented with the capabilities from its own site (COR/ISO 45001 audits, incidents and learning teams, permits and LOTO, training, contractors, emergency alerts) and clear CTAs to the product site. Occupational vision is framed in HSE language: hazard assessment, PPE compatibility, CSA Z94.3 markings, and no compliance conferral.
**Gaps:** no SafetyOS screenshots on the corporate site (by design, since the product site owns them). Industry pages would benefit from real examples once pilots exist.

## 3. Eye-care professional

**Can a clinician understand Mires?** Yes. The Mires summary covers scheduling and recall, exam/EHR, AHCIP and private billing, TELUS eClaims, dispensary and inventory, and the patient portal, all taken from the live Mires site, with a direct demo route.
**Gaps:** clinicians may also look for VPI's clinical partnership model (for example, a future network with Lunettes Emporium). That story is deliberately minimal until it's real.

## 4. Investor / strategic partner

**Can they understand what VPI owns and where it's going?** Yes. The About page shows the six-part ecosystem with status, the brand architecture (VPI, SafetyOS™, Mires™, Command Centre internal, Lunettes Emporium future) and the canonical current-versus-planned table. The Technology section proves engineering capability.
**Gaps (material for this audience):** no leadership bios, no founding date, no traction metrics, and no partner logos. **None of these can be added without owner-supplied facts** (backlog CONT-1, CONT-2).

## 5. Prospective employee

**Can they understand the company?** Mostly. Story, mission, values and "what we're building" are clear, and "Introduce yourself" goes to Careers on the contact form.
**Gaps:** no team page, no culture or working-model information, and no open roles (backlog CONT-4).

## Truthfulness check

| Rule from the brief | Status |
|---|---|
| No services claimed as available when they are not | ✅ Every service carries a status. Mobile clinics are "Planned · not yet operating". Clinical services are future tense everywhere |
| No invented customers, case studies, testimonials, statistics, clinicians, certifications, approvals, logos, deployments, pricing or partnerships | ✅ Enforced by the claim-guard test |
| No national-coverage claim | ✅ "Based in Edmonton, Alberta … designed to scale" |
| Lunettes Emporium not presented as open | ✅ One mention on About: "not yet open" |
| Command Centre not exposed | ✅ Described as internal. No link to `app.visionperformanceinc.ca` (tested) |
| Working functionality preserved | ✅ Portal, staff dashboard, all enquiry APIs (contact/mobile/corporate) retained. The shop quote flow is replaced by the Style Picker, which feeds the enquiry |

## Remaining gaps (summary)

1. Owner-supplied facts: leadership, founding date, privacy officer, phone number confirmation.
2. Real content: the six resource guides.
3. Post-deploy: Search Console, field Web Vitals, cross-browser and screen-reader passes, and a real enquiry end to end.
4. Supplier catalogue for the Style Picker once a safety-eyewear supplier is signed.
