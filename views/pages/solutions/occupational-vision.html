Why vision belongs in your safety program

Most jobs depend on vision, but it's rarely managed like other parts of health and safety. Prescriptions go out of date. Safety eyewear gets chosen without considering the prescription, and prescription eyewear without considering the hazard. Screen-heavy roles are left to individual workers to sort out.

An occupational vision program brings these pieces into one employer-run process, with clear responsibilities and a record of what was done.

What an occupational vision program is not

It doesn't replace your hazard assessment, your OH&S obligations or a worker's own eye-care provider. It organizes the vision-related parts of your program so they are handled consistently.

Program components

What a VPI program is designed to include

Role and hazard mapping

Identify the visual demands and eye hazards of each role, using your existing hazard assessments.

Eligibility and authorization

Decide who is covered, what the employer funds and how requests are approved.

Prescription safety eyewear

Eyewear selected against the hazard, with the markings your program requires.

Visual ergonomics

Guidance for screen-intensive, control-room and driving roles.

Worker education

Plain-language guidance on eye protection, eye strain and when to seek care.

Records and reporting

Program-level records for your HSE team, with personal health information kept out of employer reports.

How it would work

From first conversation to a running program

  1. Consultation

    We learn about your workforce, sites, shift patterns and existing safety program.

  2. Program design

    Coverage, eligibility, eyewear requirements and approval steps are agreed and written down.

  3. Rollout

    Workers learn how the program works and how to use it.

  4. Eyewear & referral

    Eligible workers are fitted, or referred on for clinical care when needed.

  5. Review

    Program records are reviewed with you, and the program is adjusted as roles and sites change.
